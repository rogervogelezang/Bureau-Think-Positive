The best way to identify the causes of problem behaviour and hard-to-understand behaviour is by asking the right questions.

To clarify the situation, you need questions that together build a complete picture of what might be going on, and that generate leads for further investigation.

- Questions about the behaviour displayed during the agitation: what does it look like? Is it directed at fellow clients, carers or objects? And more questions besides.
- Questions about the history: since when has this been happening? How did it start? What was the situation like then, and what is it like now?
- Questions about the care recipient's clinical picture: what are the limitations? What are they struggling with, and what are the disorders and stressors involved?
- Questions about coping capacity and burden: what can the care recipient normally cope with, what are their coping strategies, and are these still working?

In short, all kinds of questions.

Think Positive has lined up the right questions — questions that lead to answers. Not just any questions.

Besides mastering this methodical way of tackling problem behaviour and hard-to-understand behaviour (the theory), the training also leaves room to bring in cases you're struggling with (the practice). This link between practice and theory makes the training a practical tool to work with.
