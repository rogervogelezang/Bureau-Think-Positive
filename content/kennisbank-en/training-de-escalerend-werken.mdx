De-escalation is the art of calming an agitated care recipient.

But how do you calm a tense care recipient? Often, people fall back on the so-called 'as needed' option — a lorazepam, oxazepam or similar medication. But have you ever considered the countless options you have at your disposal to give the care recipient what he needs, without medication?

Think Positive has selected, from practice, the fourteen most useful methodical interventions you can use to keep a care recipient's rising emotions in check. These techniques have been brought together in the PINTA, VUUR and TRAAN series.

These interventions come straight from daily practice, are easy to learn and highly de-escalating.

Some of these interventions, such as 'encouraging' or 'offering comfort and closeness', are so self-evident that they are often used unconsciously or even forgotten altogether. You will learn to apply these interventions consciously, so that you learn to de-escalate rising emotions in a completely natural way.

Other interventions are less obvious, but just as easy to learn and highly effective in practice.

After completing this training, you will be amazed at your own ability to address inappropriate, undesirable or inadequate behaviour with de-escalating, supportive interventions.
