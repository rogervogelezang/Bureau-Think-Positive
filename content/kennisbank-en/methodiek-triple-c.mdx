Triple-C is a treatment model for people with an intellectual disability who have behavioural issues or psychological problems, developed by Hans van Wouwe and Dick van de Weerd within ASVZ. The three C's of Triple-C stand for **Client, Coach and Competence**. Unlike other treatment models, Triple-C does not focus on managing problem behaviour, but on 'restoring ordinary life'. Support workers, managers, orthopedagogues and doctors work together to build an unconditional supportive relationship with clients.

Based on that relationship, clients take part in a meaningful daily programme together with support workers. This builds their self-confidence and helps them develop competencies that have a therapeutic effect. In other words: building relationships and competencies reduces stress and problem behaviour. A normal, dignified environment invites normal human behaviour. The reverse is also true: place people in an inhumane situation — a seclusion room, for example — and you cannot expect them to display normal, human behaviour.

## The tip of the iceberg

In our view, problem behaviour is the tip of the iceberg — an expression of underlying issues. That's why we don't think it makes sense to suppress problem behaviour with protocols, medication, restraint or seclusion. Instead, we create an unconditional supportive relationship and offer clients opportunities and challenges: tasks, responsibilities and room for their own initiative. This gives clients more experiences of success, more self-confidence, and helps them develop their competencies. As a result, the problem behaviour fades into the background — and, more importantly, the underlying issues that cause it diminish.

## Central to the method

- We build an unconditional supportive relationship with our clients.
- We provide a meaningful daily structure.
- We don't focus on the problem behaviour.

Triple-C calls for thinking differently, looking differently and acting differently. Because the human needs of our clients always come first (thinking differently), we see problem behaviour as the tip of the iceberg (looking differently), and we approach our clients differently as a result (acting differently).
