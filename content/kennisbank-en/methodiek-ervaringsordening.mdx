The Meer Mens method was developed within Stichting Prisma for the target group of people with a severe intellectual disability, people with dementia and people with acquired brain injury. This is a group of clients who find it difficult to express themselves and who depend on others for almost all daily activities. The method gives care providers practical tools to get to know these clients better and to align care and support more closely with what they actually need. The aim of the method is to give this group of highly care-dependent clients more control over their own lives, thereby improving their quality of life.

The heart of the method is the care contact. After all, caring for someone always means 'making contact with that person'. Whatever kind of care you provide, caring for someone is impossible without that contact, without interaction. The quality of care therefore stands or falls with the quality of the care contact. That is what the Meer Mens method is about: optimising the care contact you have with the people you care for.

Through the method, which is based on the theory of Experience Ordering by Dr D. Timmers-Huigens and underpinned by a methodical way of working, staff, family and the wider network learn to better recognise the (complex) support needs, wishes and abilities of the individual person with a disability. They are given practical tools to respond to this effectively, purposefully, methodically and efficiently. Particular attention is paid to how support can be tailored to the client's experience ordering (co-regulation). What makes the method distinctive is that it is introduced into the field step by step: every staff member gets started straight away and, step by step and with support from the case manager, gains better insight into the client. Every step can be applied directly in practice.

## Approach

The components of experience ordering and co-regulation are explained, after which a profile sketch is completed. The profile sketch is an important tool for gaining insight into the way clients order their experiences. The concept of basic security and the factors that influence it are explained. For people with a disability, a number of the conditions for basic security are under pressure.

A checklist of points for attention is completed as a tool for gaining more clarity on this. A behavioural interpretation is also drawn up, which helps to better understand communication with the client in question.

After these steps, a joint picture is drawn up and the outlook is determined together.

Within the method, three long-term goals are used: broadening and deepening, stabilisation and maintenance, and dealing with declining abilities. The working goal, together with any sub-goals, is established, and the approach is described for each of the three goals. This methodical approach, combined with an accompanying reporting system, produces a clear picture of the client's own choices, working goals and preferences.

From there, this picture can be deepened further by offering activities that connect with the client's world of experience.
