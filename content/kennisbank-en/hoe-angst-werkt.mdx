Aggression and anger usually stem from fear. The basic emotion of fear, and the responses it triggers, are generated and directed by the amygdala. The amygdala, also known as the amygdaloid nucleus, is part of the oldest part of the brain: the limbic system. This system acts as a kind of emotional sentry — the only thing that matters is survival. When danger threatens, this sentry takes charge so we can act immediately. Adrenaline, noradrenaline and cortisol are released into the body to allow us to flee, fight, or freeze and not respond at all.

At the same time, our capacity for thought shuts down: the neocortex, also known as the rational brain. In threatening situations, after all, there's no time to sensibly weigh up the best plan of action. A fast response from the amygdala therefore allows us to avoid danger before we even realise we're in that kind of situation.

Good to know.
