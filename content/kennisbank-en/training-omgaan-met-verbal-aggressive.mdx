As a care professional, you may be confronted with verbal aggression — think of swearing, provoking, humiliating, stalking, insulting, sexual innuendo, intimidating and coercing, to name just a few examples.

Verbal aggression doesn't only come from the client themselves, but also from angry family members who come storming in.

Research has shown that 80 per cent of care professionals and carers are confronted with this.

If you don't know exactly how to handle it, it can seriously get in the way of doing your job. It can even make you ill, with all the consequences that entails.

Stay ahead of verbal aggression and, during this training, learn:

- the difference between frustration aggression, instrumental aggression and explosive aggression
- to keep your emotions in check when confronted with verbal aggression
- to stand up for yourself by using the right interventions and communication techniques, think of de-escalating interventions such as active listening, distraction and positive reframing, or confronting and boundary-setting interventions such as giving a stop signal, reflecting feelings, mirroring, setting boundaries, indicating consequences and following through on consequences
- to shift the atmosphere so that you can move forward again with the young person and/or the family

This training covers two half-days and is also available as a course (one half-day) or workshop (two half-days) for vocational (mbo) or higher professional (hbo) care programmes.
