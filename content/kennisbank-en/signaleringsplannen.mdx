A signalling plan is a list of signals you can observe in the care recipient, from which you can work out how he's doing: is he calm or tense, is he very agitated, and is there a risk of losing control?

Based on the mood, expressed in phases in the signalling plan, you as a care worker can respond to the care recipient's needs and consider which interventions to take. This way, as a care worker, you always stay one step ahead of an escalation. Very handy, in other words.

The signalling plan — in mental health care (GGZ), a distinction is made between a signalling plan and a crisis plan — is not just useful for the care worker; it also gives the care recipient tools for managing his own unrest.

## Example: signalling and intervention plan for attachment issues

### Phase 1 — Mood: relaxed

**Signals**

- The care recipient looks relaxed, with an open expression and a smile.
- The care recipient seeks attention in a positive way (he talks about what's on his mind).
- The care recipient shows interest in staff and fellow residents.
- The care recipient responds well to feedback and instructions.
- The care recipient is helpful.
- The care recipient asks for and gives affection (hugs).
- The care recipient has a good overview and can carry out his tasks without difficulty.
- The care recipient's movements are calm.

**Interventions**

- Welcome the care recipient with an open attitude.
- Give the care recipient room to tell his story, listen to what he has to say, and let him know you're there for him.
- Accept his hugs without hesitation.
- Involve the care recipient in the group process as much as possible.
- Give positive feedback on every desirable behaviour you see in him.
- If the care recipient finds it hard to accept compliments, tell him he has earned the compliment, that he deserves it.
- Use the 'sandwich message' technique when correcting him.
