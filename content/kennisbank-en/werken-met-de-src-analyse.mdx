Agitation, problem behaviour and behaviour that is difficult to understand (so-called hard-to-understand behaviour) can cause real concern for a number of people. First and foremost for the care recipient themselves, who has a support need that carers don't understand, and secondly for the carers, who don't know what to do with the incomprehensible behaviour and, as a result, often become hesitant to act.

In the moment itself, while this behaviour is happening, you can follow the protocols and signalling plans agreed on together. But what if the behaviour follows a pattern of its own and can't be caught by this safety net?

When evaluating this behaviour, the SRC method can offer a solution.

## The method

During a training course you learn the basic principles of this method, which is based on the premise that all behaviour serves a particular purpose. The letters SRC stand for: Situation, Response, Consequence.

You learn to objectively observe and describe the (crisis) situation that arose (S), the associated incomprehensible behaviour of the care recipient (R), and the interventions used by the carer (C).

You then learn to examine the possible connections between these three, in order to look for recognisable patterns in behaviour.

This creates clarity about the cause and effect of particular behaviour: which interventions had the right effect, and which had the opposite effect? The aim is to achieve better alignment in guidance and treatment.

This way of analysing behaviour is a must for evaluations and reports on escalations and crisis situations.

## An example

It's Monday evening, 10.30pm, at care home Het Oosten. The house is quiet; almost all the residents are already asleep. The nurse has put Mrs Janssen to bed and turns off the light in her room (S). Mrs Janssen starts to cry — strange, because she never does that (R). The nurse comes back to Mrs Janssen to reassure her (C). Mrs Janssen cries even harder (R). A little later, the nurse notices that the family photo Mrs Janssen is very attached to has fallen off the wall. She hangs the photo back up (C). Mrs Janssen now nods contentedly and falls asleep shortly afterwards (R).
