Behaviour is not simply strange. In the eyes of someone whose behaviour is hard to understand, what he displays is often very useful, because it is functional for him.

The client with behaviour that is hard to understand is trying to achieve something with it — in fact, he has often succeeded, and that is precisely why he once made this inappropriate behaviour his own. It serves a purpose. It serves a need. If the client manages to get what he wants, then his behaviour is useful and effective for him. If he does not, then we are looking at a support need.

The art lies in looking "through the behaviour" to uncover the client's intention: his goal or his support need. Only then can you truly begin to help, care or guide.

Fortunately, we understand this now, which is why the term "hard-to-understand behaviour" has since been given a different name.
