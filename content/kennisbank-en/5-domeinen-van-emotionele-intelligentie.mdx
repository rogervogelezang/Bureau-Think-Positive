Emotional intelligence has five domains:

- Knowledge of your own emotions.
- Regulating and managing emotions.
- Self-motivation.
- Recognising others' emotions.
- Dealing with others and social skills.

## Knowledge of your own emotions

Knowledge of your own emotions is about self-awareness: recognising a feeling the moment it comes into play. When someone cannot recognise their own feelings, they have less control over them. Recognising your own emotions forms an important foundation for emotional intelligence. Feelings usually weigh heavily in decisions, which is why it's essential to be aware of your own emotions. People with greater emotional sensitivity turn out to manage their lives better than others, for example when making choices about study, work or relationships. Psychoanalysts call this capacity "the observing ego".

## Regulating and managing emotions

This concerns emotional self-control: handling feelings in a way that contributes positively to life. People who cope poorly with fear, depression or irritation have less energy available for productive purposes. Awareness of emotions is the first step towards regulation. People with good emotional self-management and impulse control turn out to be more productive.

## Self-motivation

This is the ability to motivate yourself. Emotions can be used positively for personal growth. Hopeful and optimistic people overcome fear and low spirits more easily. Relevant factors include: seeing difficulties as challenges, impulse control, and delaying immediate gratification in favour of long-term goals.

## Recognising others' emotions

Empathy means: the ability to empathise and to recognise feelings in others. Empathetic people pick up on the feelings and needs of others, which makes those people feel understood.

## Dealing with others

This concerns handling other people's emotions. Anyone who can regulate their own emotions has more influence over how others feel. Social skills include: being attentive to others' motives, finding personal common ground, collaborating, negotiating and organising. This skill makes people good leaders and well liked.
