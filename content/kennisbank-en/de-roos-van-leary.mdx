Leary's Rose (1957) is a communication model that simplifies interaction patterns between people, making them easier to understand. The Rose is built around a dominance axis (vertical) and an affection axis (horizontal). The vertical axis runs between the extremes of above (A) and below (B); the horizontal axis runs between opposed (O) and cooperative (C). Together, the two axes divide the Rose into four quadrants of behaviour. Within each quadrant, Leary added a further distinction, giving eight different behaviours in total. Important: everyone can display all of these behaviours — it isn't a personality typology.

## How the model works

The model is based on the following natural reactions to behaviour:

1. Cooperative behaviour from one person invites cooperation from the other.
2. Oppositional behaviour from one person invites opposition from the other.
3. Dominant behaviour from one person invites dependence from the other.
4. Dependent behaviour from one person invites dominance from the other.

**Tip:** print out Leary's Rose and fold the circle in half along the horizontal axis. The behaviours that naturally trigger one another will then line up exactly.

## How you can use it

When supporting people, and in working together with colleagues, you'll come across interaction patterns in the above-opposed quadrant and/or the below-opposed quadrant of the Rose. The art of a professional intervention lies in responding to the other person's behaviour deliberately — sometimes counter-intuitively. If you want to invite cooperative behaviour, you need to show helpful behaviour yourself, even when the other person is being defiant. This way, you can bend the other person's behaviour from the left half (opposed) towards the right half (cooperative) of the circle.

Using the Rose can help you analyse a stuck, negative pattern of reactions, for example during peer supervision. It's important to know that achieving behavioural change takes time. If your intervention doesn't have the desired effect straight away, stay true to your chosen approach and don't let yourself be tempted into an obvious counter-reaction. Give the other person time to change along with you.
