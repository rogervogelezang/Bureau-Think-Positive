## Principles of Gentle Teaching

Gentle Teaching is based on the Psychology of Mutual Interdependence, developed by the American John McGee. According to this psychology, every person needs to live in connection with others, in a reciprocal and equal relationship. Following on from this, it is fundamentally important for people to feel embedded in a safe and caring community, and to be able to put their own qualities to use for the benefit of that community.

The relationship that forms the basis for social development is called companionship, and it rests on four pillars:

- feeling safe with the other person
- feeling loved by the other person
- feeling and expressing love towards the other person
- feeling connected to the other person

It is essential that this relationship is unconditional. In other words, it does not depend on the person's behaviour or mood.

For people who, for whatever reason, depend on care workers for their daily wellbeing, it is important that they experience this kind of relationship with them. That is only possible if the care worker does not approach the client from a distant, 'professional' stance, but instead engages with the client out of personal involvement and care, and handles this in a sensible way. *(gentleteaching.nl)*

Gentle Teaching is a wonderful method for supporting people with intellectual disabilities who display serious problem behaviour, because with Gentle Teaching you look beyond the undesirable behaviour, to the person behind it.

By building a relationship with the person behind the behaviour, you work on connection and create openings to support the care recipient's development.
