A handy tool to offer your care recipient, for example to help build a stronger self-image or to draw on during unsettled times.

Affirmations are positive statements that you turn into (new) beliefs about yourself. You do this by repeating these positive statements to yourself, often. The result is that you start thinking differently about yourself, feel stronger, and become open to new experiences that fit your new beliefs. Life will start confirming these new beliefs for you.

An affirmation must always be in the present tense. You state your goal as if it is already true. It's not about yesterday or tomorrow, but about the here and now.

You also phrase your affirmation as if you already have it or already are it. So you say: "Every day, I have more and more self-confidence."

Don't use negating words in affirmations. So no "I am not anxious" or "I no longer have a headache" — this kind of affirmation actually backfires. The negating words "not" and "no longer" make no impression on our subconscious, but the subject of an affirmation does stick. If you affirm "I am not anxious", your subconscious mainly hears the word "anxious". The result is even more anxiety.

Always work with affirmations that feel believable to you, otherwise they won't work. If you have money worries, for example, it's better to affirm "even though I sometimes struggle with my finances, my financial life is becoming more and more stable" than "I am as rich as a king".

Another example: if you're feeling miserable, it's better to affirm "despite this cloudy period, I still see bright spots and anchors every day" than "I feel fantastic".

You can have your care recipient work on new affirmations using what's known as an affirmation journal: a notebook containing the affirmations to be learned. The care recipient then reads out their (new) affirmations every morning. Give it a try — the affirmation journal gives your care recipient something to hold on to and a source of support.
